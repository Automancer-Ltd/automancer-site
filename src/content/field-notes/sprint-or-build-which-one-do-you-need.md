You have a problem that eats hours every week, and you've heard two different words for fixing it: a Sprint and a Build. They are not the same size of job, and picking the wrong one costs you either money or time.

Here is the short version. A Workflow Sprint automates one to three workflows you can already describe, and they go live in roughly two weeks. A Build is custom software for something a spreadsheet and a few rules can no longer hold.

## What is the difference between a Sprint and a Build?

A Sprint is for work you already understand. You know the steps, you know who does them, and the pain is the repetition. We take one to three of those workflows and automate them end to end, on tools you already use where we can. Sprints start from £1,950.

A Build is for when the thing you need doesn't exist yet. A portal, an internal platform, a compliance system, an agent that reads, decides and acts. It is designed around how you work, delivered in governed stages so you see it working before it goes live. Builds start from £4,500.

Both are from prices. If your job is bigger than the price suggests, we tell you before we start, not after.

## How do I tell which one my problem is?

Try to say it as one sentence: "When X happens, do Y."

*When a customer reorders, create the invoice and email it.* That's a Sprint.
*When leave is requested, route it to the approver, put approved leave on the calendar, and record the decision.* Also a Sprint.

Now try the other kind. Does the thing need its own data model? Its own users, each seeing different things? Permissions that change by role? Rules that interact with each other and with history? Then you are not describing a workflow. You are describing a system, and that is a Build.

Three quick checks:

1. **Can you list the steps without arguing about them?** If your team disagrees on who approves what, no software settles that. Settle it first.
2. **Does it live inside a tool you already use?** If it needs a new home, someone has to build the home too.
3. **Does it need to remember things over months?** Records, history, an audit trail. That points to a Build.

## Can one client need both?

Yes, and it is common. Our largest published piece of work, for a ~600-person supported-living care provider, has both kinds in it. The core of it is a platform with a canonical people registry, identity matching and an append-only audit trail. That is a Build. Around it, still in active build, sit smaller modules such as a mileage-claims app and leave requests, which are the kind of job you can describe as "when X, do Y". You can read how that work was broken into governed stages in the [care provider case study](/work/care-provider-transformation).

The point is that the size of the job decides the type, not the size of the company. A big organisation can have a small problem. A small one can have a problem that needs real software.

Pick the one workflow that annoys you most. Write it as "when X happens, do Y". If you can, it is probably a Sprint, and you can scope it in an afternoon. If you find yourself describing users, permissions and history, it is a Build, and it is worth doing in stages.
